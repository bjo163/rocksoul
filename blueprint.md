## 1. Executive vision

Rocksoul is an evolving intelligence runtime: a stable, portable core designed to progressively acquire capabilities and operate across different applications, environments, and interfaces.

It begins as one native Rust executable communicating through a CLI. It is not initially a dashboard, chatbot wrapper, automation suite, or collection of independent agents.

The long-term aspiration is **Artificial General Intelligence (AGI)**: increasingly general, transferable, reliable, and accountable intelligence. AGI is an aspiration and research direction, not a claim about the current system.

Rocksoul must not be architecturally locked to one model provider, interface, application type, or execution environment.

### North-star statement

**One Core. Infinite Evolution.**

- One identity and one coherent runtime.
- Many capabilities, tools, workflows, and interfaces.
- Growth through measured experience and validated improvement.
- Stable foundations, controlled evolution, and recoverability.

## 2. Foundational vision: AGI · ASMA-99 · Four Kitab

The strategic vision has three connected foundations. They have different responsibilities and must not be conflated.

### 2.1 AGI — intelligence aspiration

AGI is the long-term aspiration for general-purpose intelligence. Rocksoul should progressively develop and evaluate:

- Perception and multimodal understanding.
- Memory, learning, and knowledge integration.
- Reasoning, planning, and decision-making.
- Execution and reliable tool use.
- Language and interaction.
- Safety, governance, and calibrated uncertainty.
- Creativity, transfer, and adaptation.
- Metacognition and self-evaluation.
- Social cognition and collaboration.
- Controlled capability improvement.

No single scalar score, model self-report, or successful chat response demonstrates AGI. Report capability vectors, evidence, uncertainty, sample counts, and safety gates.

### 2.2 ASMA-99 — 99-name-inspired capability architecture

ASMA-99 is the product's respectful naming and inspiration framework for **99 distinct technical intelligence domains and 99 corresponding stats**.

The required architecture is:

**99 Asma references → 99 individual domains → 99 individual technical stats.**

This is a one-to-one mapping. It is not nine domains containing eleven stats each.

Each of the 99 slots must have its own stable identity and technical definition. Optional navigation families may group domains for presentation, but families are not domains and must never reduce or replace the 99-domain model.

Each domain/stat record must define:

1. Stable slot number and immutable identifier (01–99).
2. Asma reference and source/provenance metadata.
3. Domain key and domain name.
4. One corresponding technical stat key and label.
5. Operational capability definition.
6. Evaluation protocol and benchmark references.
7. Minimum sample requirement.
8. Scoring rubric and version.
9. Evidence references, confidence, and timestamp.
10. Review status and change history.

**Religious boundary:** Asmaul Husna is a respectful naming/inspiration layer only. Rocksoul does not possess, embody, measure, or claim to reproduce any divine attribute. Technical stats measure bounded software capabilities, not Allah, divine qualities, a person's iman, spiritual rank, or divine approval. The 99-name enumeration, Arabic, transliteration, glosses, and editorial associations require appropriate independent religious and language review.

#### 99 stats scoring

- There are exactly 99 individual stats.
- Each stat is independently evaluated.
- The existing engineering rubric uses a 0–100 score and a separate 0–100 confidence value. The number 99 describes the count of domains/stats, not the maximum score.
- All stats begin as **unrated**, not zero.
- A score requires a trusted evaluator, reproducible evidence, benchmark/rubric version, timestamp, sample count, and confidence.
- The evaluated system/model cannot authorize its own score.
- Safety, privacy, and permission failures are hard gates and cannot be averaged away.

#### Migration from the current catalogue

The current repository catalogue contains 99 unique technical stat entries grouped into nine technical group labels of eleven entries each. This is the existing implementation baseline, not the final 99-domain architecture.

The target schema must promote each of the 99 slots into its own domain with exactly one corresponding stat. The existing nine group labels may be retained as optional `family` or `category` metadata for navigation only. They must not remain the authoritative domain count. This migration must be versioned, tested, and preserve stable slot IDs and historical evaluations.

### 2.3 Four Kitab Allah — knowledge foundation

Rocksoul's religious knowledge foundation covers four revealed books:

| Priority in the Rocksoul Islamic knowledge framework | Book | Prophet |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | **Al-Qur'an** | Prophet Muhammad ﷺ |
| Supporting reference | Taurat (Tawrat) | Prophet Musa |
| Supporting reference | Zabur (Zabur) | Prophet Dawud |
| Supporting reference | Injil | Prophet Isa |

**Al-Qur'an is the primary and highest reference in Rocksoul's Islamic knowledge framework.** The other three books remain part of the Four Kitab knowledge foundation and are handled with respect, attribution, and historical context. This product hierarchy describes the system's Islamic knowledge policy; it does not erase the identity of the other revealed books.

The system must distinguish:
- The revealed book as a matter of religious belief.
- Extant textual witnesses and manuscripts.
- Historical editions and published texts.
- Translations and transliterations.
- Passages, claims, citations, and interpretations.
- Scholarly views, historical evidence, and model-generated commentary.

A current textual witness or translation must never be falsely represented as the original revelation without evidence.

#### Knowledge architecture

`BOOK → TEXTUAL WITNESS → EDITION / TRANSLATION → SECTION → PASSAGE → CLAIM → SOURCE CHECK → INTERPRETATION`

Every stored item must carry provenance: source, edition, language, translator/editor where known, location or passage identifier, retrieval date where relevant, and verification status.

Knowledge rules:
- Al-Qur'an is the primary reference in the Islamic knowledge hierarchy.
- Preserve the original text and distinguish it from translations.
- Never fabricate verses, quotations, citations, or textual parallels.
- Separate source text from translation, commentary, and interpretation.
- Record disagreements and uncertainty rather than silently flattening them.
- Do not let model-generated claims become verified knowledge automatically.
- Preserve context and attribution for all four books.

## 4. RID — identity and progression

RID is Rocksoul's persistent instance identity and developmental profile.

RID keeps distinct concepts separate:

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Identity | Stable identity of one Rocksoul instance |
| Age | Elapsed time since instance initialization |
| XP | Append-only record of verified progression events |
| Level | Deterministic progression derived from cumulative XP |
| 99 Stats | Independently measured technical capabilities |
| Confidence | Strength/uncertainty of evaluation evidence |
| Evidence | Auditable support for XP and stat changes |
| Version | Runtime, schema, rubric, and level-curve versions |

### XP integrity

- XP is awarded only through trusted, independently verifiable events.
- Ordinary chat, token count, uptime, message count, and model self-report do not earn XP.
- Events are append-only, uniquely identified, bounded, and idempotent.
- Replaying the same event cannot award XP twice.
- Reusing an event ID with different data is rejected.
- Each award records source category, timestamp, evidence reference, and policy version.
- XP and Level are separate from all 99 stat scores.

### Level integrity

Levels are derived deterministically from cumulative XP using a versioned curve. The current repository curve is version 1: level N is reached at `50 × (N - 1) × N` cumulative XP (100 XP for level 2, 300 for level 3, 600 for level 4).

Changing the curve requires versioning and migration rules. Do not silently rewrite historical XP or levels.

### Anti-overlap rules

- **RID** owns instance identity, age, XP history, and level.
- **99 Stats** own capability measurements.
- **Evaluation Engine** owns benchmark execution and evidence.
- **Memory** owns persistent records and retrieval.
- **Evolution Engine** owns improvement proposals and candidate lineage.

No metric should be counted twice under different names.

## 6. Self-evolution architecture

Self-evolution means controlled improvement, not unrestricted self-editing.

Required lifecycle:

`OBSERVE → IDENTIFY LIMITATION → PROPOSE → DESIGN → GENERATE CANDIDATE → SANDBOX → TEST → EVALUATE → APPROVE → INTEGRATE → VERIFY`

Requirements:
- Capture an immutable known-good baseline.
- Create an isolated candidate workspace or worktree.
- Apply bounded changes with explicit scope.
- Build, lint, test, and run task-specific evaluations.
- Compare candidate and baseline under matched conditions.
- Preserve source lineage, logs, reports, and artifacts.
- Require approval according to risk and policy.
- Promote only validated changes.
- Support rollback and reproducible rebuilds.
- Never use generated code executing in-process as the security boundary.
- Never overwrite the only known-good executable before validation.

Conceptual evolution levels:

| Level | Capability |
|---|---|
| E0 | Use existing capabilities |
| E1 | Improve instructions and configuration proposals |
| E2 | Create skills and workflows |
| E3 | Create tools and projects in approved workspaces |
| E4 | Develop isolated subsystems |
| E5 | Propose core modifications |
| E6 | Integrate validated improvements under governance |

These levels describe capability maturity, not RID user levels.

## 7. Eight-stage product roadmap

Stages are capability milestones, not promises that AGI will be achieved.

### Stage 0 — SEED
- One native Rust executable.
- CLI and REPL.
- `init`, `status`, `doctor`.
- Local model configuration.
- OpenAI-compatible provider and Ollama compatibility.
- Portable root storage.
- RID identity, age, XP/Level foundation, and 99 unrated stat slots.
- Cognitive Cockpit as an optional terminal interface.
- Deterministic offline Core Integrity evaluation.

**Exit:** portable executable, reproducible build, stable initialization, diagnostics, and verified core tests.

### Stage 1 — COGNITION
- Structured turn lifecycle.
- Planning and bounded reasoning.
- Reflection with explicit evidence limitations.
- Context management.
- Cancellation and budgets.
- Independent task acceptance checks.

**Exit:** bounded tasks can be planned, executed through approved services, and verified through observed evidence.

### Stage 2 — MEMORY
- Versioned persistent memory.
- Episodic, semantic, procedural, working, and derived records.
- Provenance and explicit approval.
- Search, export, deletion, retention, migration, and recovery.
- Tested retrieval and context injection.

**Exit:** cross-session memory remains inspectable, permissioned, recoverable, and evidence-aware.

### Stage 3 — CAPABILITIES
- Typed capability/tool contracts.
- Registry and discovery.
- Read-only tools first.
- Permission checks, audit events, timeouts, and sandboxing.
- Explicit approval for writes and external egress.

**Exit:** capabilities are composable and bounded by policy.

### Stage 4 — BUILDER
- Project scaffolding and templates.
- Workspace-scoped file generation.
- Code analysis and build orchestration.
- Tests and acceptance criteria.
- Change summaries and failure reporting.

**Exit:** generated projects can be built and validated reproducibly.

### Stage 5 — EVALUATION & EVOLUTION
- ASMA-99 domain/stat schema migration to 99 individual domains.
- Trusted stat evaluators and benchmark coverage.
- Sandboxed candidate changes.
- Baseline comparison and lineage.
- Approval, promotion, and rollback.
- Memory and transfer evaluation.

**Exit:** improvement is demonstrated through reproducible evidence and safe promotion.

### Stage 6 — WORKFORCE
- Multi-agent delegation where it offers measured benefit.
- Bounded tasks, tools, contexts, and budgets.
- Shared artifacts and controlled memory access.
- Coordination, conflict handling, and supervision.

**Exit:** multiple workers coordinate without losing traceability or governance.

### Stage 7 — EXPANSION
- Desktop, web, API, automation, and distributed execution.
- Common core contracts and identity.
- Infrastructure integrations through explicit adapters.
- Reliable operation across supported environments.

**Exit:** multiple interfaces and environments use the same coherent runtime.

## 9. Portable-first filesystem

All mutable Rocksoul configuration and runtime data must remain under the Rocksoul installation root beside the executable. Do not scatter operational state into AppData, Documents, or the user profile.

Target layout (introduced progressively):

```text
rocksoul/
├── rocksoul.exe
├── .env
├── config/
│   └── config.toml
├── data/
│   ├── identity/
│   ├── memory/
│   ├── evaluations/
│   ├── rid/
│   └── knowledge/
├── capabilities/
├── workspace/
├── evolution/
│   ├── proposals/
│   ├── experiments/
│   └── history/
├── cache/
├── logs/
└── backups/
```

Requirements:
- Portable ZIP can run from a user-chosen root.
- Installer preserves user configuration and data on upgrade.
- Secrets are excluded from Git and logs.
- Explicit permissions protect mutable data.
- Backup, restore, validation, and schema migration are supported.
- Runtime paths are derived from the executable/root contract, not arbitrary user-profile defaults.
- Do not create unused directories before their subsystem exists.

## 12. Governance, safety, and engineering workflow

Workflow:

`IDEA → RESEARCH → SPECIFICATION → PLAN → SMALL ISSUES → FEATURE BRANCH → IMPLEMENTATION → TEST → PR → REVIEW → MAIN → RELEASE`

Branch prefixes:
- `feat-*`
- `fix-*`
- `chore-*`
- `docs-*`

Engineering rules:
- `main` remains stable.
- Changes are small, reviewable, and independently testable.
- Every feature has acceptance criteria.
- CI must pass before merge.
- Release versions, changelogs, artifacts, and checksums are traceable.
- Validate Windows and Linux where supported.
- Documentation reflects actual implementation state.
- No force-push or destructive history rewrite as a routine integration strategy.

Safety rules:
- Least privilege and explicit capability permissions.
- Sandboxed execution for generated code.
- Resource, time, and token budgets.
- Explicit approval for high-impact operations.
- Audit trails and immutable evidence references.
- Provider credential isolation.
- Recoverable data writes and rollback.
- No unrestricted shell or filesystem execution in the seed.
- No autonomous self-modification without the complete evolution pipeline.
